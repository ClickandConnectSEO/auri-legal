# Auri Care — the plan to $100k MRR

Written 2026-09-06. Revised same day when the target changed from $100k **per year** to $100k **per month**.
Owner: Auri. Build partner: Claude Code.

---

## 0. What changed, and what it costs you

The first version of this plan targeted $100,000 a year. You want $100,000 a month. That is 12x the revenue and a different kind of company.

Read these three numbers before anything else.

- **Only 5% of micro SaaS companies exceed $100k MRR.** Half plateau between $1k and $10k. Thirty percent never reach $1k.
- **Median time to $1M ARR, roughly $83k MRR, is 2 years and 9 months.** Nobody does it in a year.
- **$100k MRR is not $100k in your pocket.** At that revenue you will carry 4 to 6 people. Payroll alone runs $40-60k a month. Your take-home lands nearer $25-40k a month.

None of this makes the target wrong. It makes the plan different. You are no longer building a lifestyle product. You are building a company, and you will hire.

## 1. The one decision worth more than everything else

The original plan priced Auri at a flat $149-399 a month. At a $179 blend, $100k MRR needs **559 agencies**.

Home care software does not price flat. The industry standard is a base fee plus a per-caregiver or per-client charge. Mid-market platforms charge **$200-500 base plus $10-25 per caregiver a month**. Agencies already expect this and already budget for it.

Switch to the industry's own unit and the arithmetic changes:

| Pricing model | Blended monthly | Agencies for $100k MRR |
|---|---|---|
| Flat $179 | $179 | 559 |
| **$199 base + $12 per caregiver** | **$650** | **154** |

**Same sales effort. Roughly one third the customers.** This is the single highest-leverage change in the plan, and it costs nothing to make because you have not built anything yet.

### What the tiers look like in practice

| Agency size | Caregivers | Monthly | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner-operator | 15 | $379 | Entry. Your first 30 customers. |
| Established | 60 | $919 | The core. Most revenue comes from here. |
| Multi-location | 150 | $1,999 | Year three. Needs a salesperson. |

Nothing here is above what agencies pay today. $195 to $1,500 a month is the normal range for a small or mid-sized agency.

## 2. Net revenue retention is the actual engine

You will not reach $100k MRR by adding customers alone. You reach it by growing the accounts you already have.

At **120% net revenue retention**, 100 agencies paying $400 today pay $691 in three years with zero new sales. That compounding is what separates the 5% from the 50% who plateau.

Two things drive it, and both are already in the plan:

1. **Per-caregiver pricing.** When an agency grows from 20 caregivers to 45, your revenue grows without a conversation.
2. **A module ladder.** Sell the same buyer more product every year.

### The module ladder

| Year | Module | Added price | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Family communication | $199 + $12/caregiver | Wins private-pay clients on trust |
| 2 | Credential and expiry tracking | +$4/caregiver | An expired CNA cert stops billing |
| 3 | Call-out and shift backfill | +$6/caregiver | Coordinators work the phones at 6am. This is the deepest pain in the business. |

Year one price for a 60-caregiver agency: $919. Year three: **$1,519.** Same customer, no new acquisition cost.

## 3. The beachhead has not changed

Start where the first plan started. The wedge is still right, and it is still the cheapest way in.

**Auri turns every visit into a family update.**

A caregiver finishes a visit. The family hears nothing until someone phones the office. Agencies win private-pay clients on trust, and right now trust is a voicemail.

### v1 — four things

1. **Caregiver web app.** End of visit, under 30 seconds. Tap what happened: meals, medication prompted, mood, activity. Optional photo. Optional voice note, transcribed.
2. **Family view.** One link. No app install, no password. Today's update, a timeline, photos.
3. **Agency console.** Add clients, invite families, review flagged updates before they send. The agency logo on everything.
4. **Weekly digest email.** The retention hook, not a nice extra.

### What v1 refuses to do

Scheduling. Electronic visit verification. Payroll. Billing. Clinical charting. Medication administration records.

Each brings compliance weight and a funded incumbent (WellSky, AlayaCare, Axxess, HHAeXchange). Attack the gap, not the fortress. **This changes in year three, not before.**

### The sales line

> One extra private-pay client at 20 hours a week and $35 an hour is $36,400 a year. Auri at 60 caregivers costs $11,028 a year.

Sell revenue, not saved time.

## 4. Why home care still holds up at this scale

- Healthcare vertical SaaS churns **6-10% a year**. Marketing tools churn 18-24%. At $100k MRR, a 20% churn rate means replacing $20k of revenue annually before you grow at all.
- Vertical software retains **30-50% better** than horizontal tools at the same contract value.
- Small agencies already spend **$195-1,500 a month** on software. Mid-market pays $200-500 base plus per-caregiver fees on top.
- Roughly **33,000 home care agencies** operate in the US. The 154 you need is **0.47% of the market.**

Under half of one percent. That is the number to hold onto when the target feels impossible.

## 5. The four stages

Each stage has one job and one exit condition. Do not skip one.

### Stage 1 — Prove it (months 0-12) → $10k MRR

25 agencies, roughly $400 average. Solo, with Claude Code building.

- 20 discovery calls before any code
- v1 shipped, 3 design partners live
- First 25 paying agencies, all sold by you, by hand
- **Exit condition:** 25 paying agencies and monthly churn under 3%

Funded from your $500-2,000 budget. **This is the only stage your personal money enters.**

### Stage 2 — Repeat it (months 13-24) → $35k MRR

70 agencies, roughly $500 average. Credential module ships. First hire.

- **Hire one:** customer success and onboarding, around $20k MRR. Onboarding an agency takes hours and it does not scale on you.
- One acquisition channel proven and repeatable
- **Exit condition:** net revenue retention above 110% and a channel producing 4+ agencies a month without your direct effort

Funded by revenue. Not by you.

### Stage 3 — Scale it (months 25-42) → $100k MRR

154 agencies, roughly $650 average. Backfill module ships. Two more hires.

- **Hire two:** a salesperson at ~$35k MRR, an engineer at ~$50k MRR
- Move upmarket into multi-location and franchise groups
- **Exit condition:** $100k MRR, NRR above 120%

### Stage 4 — Decide (month 43+)

At $100k MRR you own a business worth roughly 4 to 5 times ARR. Sell, hold, or keep growing. That is a decision for a version of you with better information.

## 7. The three things most likely to stop you

### Caregiver adoption
Turnover is high, caregivers are rushed, some are low-tech. If they do not log the visit, the family view sits empty and the agency cancels in month two.

Mitigations: 30 seconds hard ceiling, no app store download, voice note fallback, and the owner mandates it as part of the visit. Ship the onboarding script as part of the product.

### Staying solo too long
The 50% who plateau at $1-10k MRR mostly plateau because the founder became the bottleneck and never hired. **Hire customer success at $20k MRR even though it feels early.** Especially then.

### Undercharging out of nerves
The instinct at the first sales call is to drop the price. Every dollar off the per-caregiver rate multiplies across every agency and every year. Hold the price. Lose the deal instead.

## 8. HIPAA is a gate, not a footnote

A visit note about a named person's health, held for a care agency, is protected health information. The agency is the covered entity. Auri is the business associate.

**Required before the first paying agency:**
- Signed business associate agreement with every agency and every infrastructure vendor
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Audit logging on every read of a client record
- Role-based access enforced in the database, not the interface
- Zero health information in application logs or analytics
- A published subprocessor list

| Path | Monthly | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **AWS under a signed BAA** | $60-120 | The agreement is free to sign. You pay for usage. More setup work. |
| Supabase Team + HIPAA add-on | $599+ | Faster to build on. Eats most of the stage 1 budget. |

**Pay a healthcare technology lawyer for a two-hour review before the first agency signs.** Budget $800-1,500.

At stage 3 you will need a SOC 2 Type II report to sell to multi-location groups. Budget $25-40k in year three. Put it in the plan now so it is not a surprise.

## 9. Acquisition

Home care is regional and referral-driven. Owners talk to each other constantly. Do not start with ads.

1. **Twenty discovery calls, before any code.** LinkedIn, title "Owner, Home Care Agency". Ask what happens after a visit ends.
2. **Three free design partners, all local.** Drive to them. Watch a caregiver use it in a real house.
3. **One case study with a number in it.** "This agency closed three more private-pay clients in 60 days."
4. **HCAOA state chapter meetings.** Where owners already gather. Sponsor one for the price of a week of ads.
5. **Franchise networks.** Home Instead, Right at Home, Comfort Keepers, Visiting Angels. Franchisee Facebook groups first, franchisor once five franchisees are happy. **A single franchisor deal is worth 50 agencies. This is the stage 3 unlock.**
6. **Referral fee.** One month of revenue per closed introduction.

## 10. Kill criteria

Written now, while honesty is still cheap.

- Fewer than **8 of 20** discovery calls ask "when do I get it" → the wedge is wrong. Change it before building.
- Design-partner caregivers not logging **3x weekly by day 30** → adoption has beaten you. Fix it or stop.
- No signed contract at the **full per-caregiver rate** by month 5 → the price or the buyer is wrong.
- Under **$3k MRR at month 12** → the channel does not work. Rethink acquisition, not the product.
- **NRR under 100% at month 24** → the ladder is not working. $100k MRR is out of reach without it. Reset the target to a strong $30-40k MRR business and say so plainly.

That last one matters most. A $35k MRR business run by three people is an excellent outcome. Reaching it and pretending it failed would be the real loss.
